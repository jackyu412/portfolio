I worked on developing spectroscopic instrumentation and experimental techniques to study ionic motion in solid-state electrolytes, with a focus on Laser-driven Ultrafast Impedance Spectroscopy (LUIS). The technique uses ultrafast laser pulses to probe charge transport and ionic dynamics on timescales inaccessible to conventional impedance spectroscopy. To investigate the coupling between lattice vibrations (phonons) and mobile ions, I designed and built a Peltier-cooled sample stage that allows measurements across a range of temperatures. By controlling the sample temperature, we can modify the initial thermal phonon population and study how temperature-dependent lattice dynamics influence ionic motion. The broader goal is to better understand the microscopic mechanisms governing ion transport in solid-state electrolytes and how they might be tuned to improve next-generation energy storage materials.
