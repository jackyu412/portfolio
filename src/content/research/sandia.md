I spent two summers working in the optoelectronics R&D lab at Sandia National Laboratories, where I gained hands-on experience designing, building, and characterizing optical systems. I prototyped a piezo-tunable Fabry–Pérot etalon for chip-scale cesium atomic clocks, built an external-cavity diode laser (ECDL) to evaluate antireflection coatings on semiconductor lasers, and worked on optical characterization of VCSELs for atomic clocks and magnetometers. This work contributed to a SPIE conference publication on amplitude and phase noise characterization of VCSELs. Alongside the optical work, I learned to design and manually machine optomechanical components using mills and lathes, as well as fabricate and assemble custom laboratory hardware.
